Light through the ages

Many stories. One celebration.

Diwali is celebrated across communities in different ways and for different reasons. These stories and customs are part of a rich, varied festival—not a single explanation shared by everyone.

Hindu tradition

Rama's return to Ayodhya

In many North Indian traditions, lamps welcome Rama, Sita and Lakshmana home after fourteen years in exile and Rama's victory over Ravana. Rows of lights turn the return into a celebration of hope and righteousness.

Hindu tradition

Krishna and Narakasura

Naraka Chaturdashi recalls Krishna's defeat of the tyrant Narakasura in traditions that tell this story. The day before the main Lakshmi Puja, it is marked in many places with early baths, lamps and joyful preparations.

Hindu tradition

Lakshmi and prosperity

For many Hindus, Lakshmi Puja honours the goddess of prosperity, abundance and good fortune. Homes and shops are cleaned and illuminated, and families pray for wellbeing in the year ahead.

Jain tradition

Mahavira's nirvana

Jains mark Diwali as the day Lord Mahavira attained nirvana. Lamps also recall the light of his teachings, and the occasion is observed with prayer, reflection and community gatherings.

Sikh tradition

Bandi Chhor Divas

Sikhs celebrate Bandi Chhor Divas, commemorating Guru Hargobind Sahib's release from imprisonment and the freedom of 52 princes. It often coincides with Diwali and is observed with prayers and illuminated gurdwaras.

Regional traditions

A festival with many local rhythms

Customs vary by region: West Bengal observes Kali Puja; Gujarat welcomes a new year after Diwali; Tamil Nadu often celebrates Naraka Chaturdashi at dawn; Maharashtra prepares faral, festive snacks. North Indian celebrations commonly centre Lakshmi Puja and Rama's return.

Five days of light

The Diwali celebrations

The sequence and observances vary by region and family. Explore some widely celebrated days.

01

Dhanteras

A day associated with wellbeing and prosperity. Families clean, light lamps, and often buy something new or precious.

02

Naraka Chaturdashi

Also called Choti Diwali in many regions. Ritual baths, lamps and preparations welcome the festive day.

03

Lakshmi Puja

The main festival night for many. Prayer, lamps, rangoli and a shared festive meal fill homes with light.

04

Govardhan Puja

In many Hindu communities, Govardhan Puja honours Krishna and the hill, with Annakut offerings of food.

05

Bhai Dooj

A day celebrating sibling bonds, with prayers, gifts and time spent together in many families.
